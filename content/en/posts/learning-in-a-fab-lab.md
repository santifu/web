When someone enters a Fab Lab for the first time, the first thing that catches their attention is not the machines but the noise. A particular noise: the sound of people thinking out loud while making things.

## Error as method

In a traditional classroom, error is something to be avoided. In a maker space, error is the method. Nobody learns to use a laser cutter without burning something first. Nobody designs a perfect part on the first try.

This inversion of error has deep pedagogical consequences. It changes the student's relationship with knowledge: from passive receiver to active researcher.

## Spaces as curriculum

The physical space of a Fab Lab is not neutral. Every visible tool is an invitation. Every project in progress is an open question. The space itself is part of the curriculum.

This connects to what Seymour Papert called *constructionism*: we learn best when we build things to share with others.
